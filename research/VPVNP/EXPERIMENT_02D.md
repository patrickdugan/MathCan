# VPVNP-02D: hyperplane cancellation and finite reduction

Registered 2026-11-24. The [02C run](https://github.com/patrickdugan/MathCan/actions/runs/45328677562) passed exact ordered-basis DP including the 02A archive comparison. Native check failed on three elaboration obligations (ZMod 2 case decidability, sign at 1+1, sum_comp namespace); this commit corrects these before the new proof is attempted.

D1: for Bits I = I → ZMod 2, define K_b={x:⟨b,x⟩=0} and O_b its complement. Show kernels uniquely determine b by evaluating coordinate units; the family of nonzero parity kernels has size |Bits I|-1. Show nonzero b gives |O_b|=|Bits I|/2 using Walsh cancellation. D2: for nonzero b,c with b≠c there exists t in K_b with ⟨c,t⟩=1. Translation by t preserves O_b and reverses the c-character. Hence sum_{x∈O_b} chi_c(x)=0; if c=b the sum is -|O_b|. D3: prove the exact final-column mean equals -1 if b=c, 0 otherwise, and the uniform sum over nonzero normals collapses to the c-fiber. Establish the elementary annihilator fact: if a functional d vanishes on K_b then d=0 or d=b. This yields independence of remaining coefficient functionals restricted to K_b when b is independent of them (the coefficient independence condition is explicit).

D4 exact finite regression: n=1..6, every b,c nonzero, check K_b uniqueness, balanced outside cardinality, cancellation and same-normal sign, and the annihilator of K_b. For independent normal sets (coordinate representatives) also check remaining functionals restrict independently to K_b. No random tuning; compare all cases exactly. Report count and deterministic artifacts.

Scope: this phase proves the parity-kernel and final-coset algebra. A later proof must still identify the span of each ordered (n-1)-frame with a unique nonzero parity kernel, show uniform fiber cardinalities for actual ordered bases, and identify restrictions with the lower-dimensional character sum. The normalized recurrence is conditional until those distributional steps are kernel checked. Do not assert a complete all-n Lean theorem on the strength of the exact DP.

# CTS-10: Lazy affine certificate compilation

Status: exact algebraic identity, online operation-count bound, synthetic development results. No neural training or paid compute. Continues CTS-09. Full warm HiGHS with inverse reuse is the comparison baseline; it obtains a basis from every solve and uses the same exact inverse-based certificate verifier.

## Mathematical result

For fixed integer normals A and b=b0+B u, write the cached active inverse as A_S^T N=q I, q>0. The primal numerator is N^T b_S, dual numerator N c. An exact certificate requires A N^T b_S <= q b and N c>=0. The identity establishes primal and dual equality on the support. For a translation factor B_j=A v_j, N^T B_{S,j}=q v_j, so the column of A N^T B_S-qB is zero. Verify that identity and omit the translation from the compiled map. The implementation retains the full factor provenance check, and reconstructs the original primal numerator when returning a value. Independently supplied RHS values fall back to direct exact transport when the affine relation fails. The cache is scoped to one fixed A, b0 and B.

With n rows, dimension d>2 and two retained factors, the counted direct feasibility scan costs D=nd scalar product terms, the reduced scan R=2n, and compilation C=3nd. These counts exclude the small inverse-side products, factor-provenance check, dual test, memory and bit complexity. Compile on eligible primal scan k=ceil(C/(D-R)); count only scans that pass exact dual feasibility and have verified affine provenance. Rejections after a primal scan count. Eviction resets the lifetime. If h<k, direct is the offline optimum; otherwise the offline optimum is C+hR. The lazy cost for h>=k is (k-1)D+C+(h-k+1)R = C+hR+(k-1)(D-R). Since (k-1)(D-R)<C, the lazy cost is at most twice the offline optimum. This is a per-cache-lifetime comparison on the counted work only, with the candidate trajectory fixed. It is not a bound on end-to-end time. The thresholds are 4 scans for d=8,16,32. The proof remains valid for general positive C and D>R>=0.

## Experiment

24 independently seeded sequences, 64 steps per sequence, dimensions 8/16/32, four normal families (dense, sparse, correlated, near-active), two RHS modes (recurring structured vs mixed structured/independent capacity shocks); 1,536 LPs. Constraints are synthetic, with fixed A and bounding rows inherited from CTS-09. Two public capacity masks partition rows by the sign of A c0 and use randomized nonnegative depth weights. A third factor is a translation. Objectives cycle through four anchors with periodic perturbations and novel objectives. Mixed schedules add independent nonnegative row offsets in the second half of each 16-step block. These are illustrative capacity constraints, not order-book snapshots or actual clearing rules. One root per family/dimension/mode cell; all are exposed development data.

Cache capacity 8, LRU eviction, at most two exact candidate attempts; ranking uses approximate normalized dual multipliers, acceptance is exact. Full reuse always solves and caches inverses; direct tests candidate feasibility through direct transport; eager compiles on the first eligible scan; lazy compiles at the threshold. Direct/eager/lazy use identical ranking and solver fallback, verified step by step. All timing includes factor provenance checks, inverse construction and solver work; generator time excluded. CPU timing is descriptive and hardware-specific. The private SciPy HiGHS interface is a prototype dependency. Infeasible or unbounded problems and singular basis recovery are outside this test. No confidence intervals or neural causality claims.

## Results

All 6,144 policy outcomes agreed on exact optimal objective value. The three caching arms have identical support, hit, fallback and iteration traces: 784 hits, 752 solver calls and 36,442 simplex iterations, versus 1,536 calls and 69,494 iterations for full warm HiGHS with inverse reuse. Eager compiles 605 maps, lazy 80. Direct, eager and lazy count 18,081,792; 21,809,920; and 16,102,656 primal+compilation scalar product terms respectively. Both affine arms additionally count 1,376,256 factor-provenance terms. Lazy reduces the principal ledger by 10.95% versus direct, or 3.33% with the provenance terms included. These are partial arithmetic ledgers, not FLOPs or wall-clock bounds.

First run totals (seconds): full 10.199, direct 8.500, eager 8.941, lazy 8.534. Reordered replay: full 10.464, direct 8.535, eager 8.962, lazy 8.640. Direct is slightly faster than lazy on both timings. Recurring mode hits 542/768; mixed mode 242/768. Timing gains reflect synthetic recurrence and may not transfer to real-market workloads.

## Verification and reproduction

All 6,144 non-timing records reproduce exactly in the replay with permuted arm order. Independent explicit exact solves reconstruct 1,536 distinct step/support pairs; 1,152 affine/direct tests and 384 non-affine fallback tests agree; 24 translation identities, 24 cutting-constraint rejections and 24 changed-normal invalidations pass. Five integer overflow fixtures pass. 15,934 finite online-bound fixtures (d=3..64, h=0..256) pass; the inequality above supplies the general mathematical argument. The independent reconstruction uses the same SymPy backend but bypasses the cached inverse path. No Lean verification claimed.

Use Python with numpy 2.3.5, scipy 1.17.0, sympy 1.14.0 (versions verified in the sandbox). All source is included, including the CTS-09 helper. To reproduce within bounded runtime, run `python cts10.py --start 0 --end 4 --out results0.json`, then ranges 4..8 and 8..12; concatenate in cell order using `summarize` and `dump`. Repeat with `--repeat 1`; compare `stable` records. Run `python cts10.py --audit-input results.json --out audit.json`. The first all-in-one attempt timed out and was discarded; the full primary and replay were each completed in three disjoint chunks. `results.json` contains the primary raw records, `summary.json` and `replay.json` contain aggregates, `audit.json` records checks. The package excludes redundant replay raw records and the aborted run. All work remains a sandbox artifact.

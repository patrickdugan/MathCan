## 03B: diagnose the effective gauge discrepancy

For a known diagonal vertex gauge G, ideal return probabilities of H and GHG† agree. With an actual device implementation, write the circuit-dependent effective map as E_G. A basis-return discrepancy measures an operational implementation difference. It cannot, by itself, identify the channel superoperator commutator or distinguish calibration drift, synthesis changes and physical gate errors.

Subtract each gauge copy's own Aer prediction from its observed return. At the four f0 offsets the gauge-minus-original differences after this adjustment are +0.0153821, −0.0162065, −0.0029841, +0.0117085. Both arms use 29 two-qubit gates at each offset and share the physical readout mapping, with circuit depths differing by up to two. The conditional independent-binomial Pearson score is 38.15 on four dimensions (nominal tail 1.04e−7). This is a descriptive fixed-model diagnostic, not a significance claim under unknown correlated device drift. Across the 24 target cases the Aer residual RMS is 0.01298, versus an average nominal counting SD of 0.00496; the excess RMS is about 0.01200. The joint chord score is 29.75 versus 11.07; posthoc inflation 2.69 would move this observation to the boundary, and must never be counted as prospective coverage.

For a fixed channel E on a basis of N vertices, define a true diagonal-gauge twirl T(E) = integral Ad_(G†) E Ad_G dG over U(1)^N. Haar invariance proves T(E) commutes with all gauge conjugations. In matrix units, E_ij for i≠j spans a distinct character space, while diagonal operators share the trivial character. Hence T(E) preserves the diagonal subspace and acts by a scalar on each off-diagonal matrix unit. Twirling removes inter-character mixing but retains gauge-invariant error. Independently recompiling GHG† generally changes E_G; such an ensemble is an engineering heuristic and is not automatically this mathematical twirl.

The wrappers around basis input/output alone are a matched circuit control; they do not constitute complete channel tomography. Interleave order and preserve per-setting metadata. Fit a modest correlated residual model only on calibration data. Compare held-out predictive coverage and phase RMSE to the frozen Q3 model. Include shot-only, diagonal inflation, and structured covariance baselines. Keep joint intervals calibrated from held-out fields, not the single Kingston outcome. Treat gauge phases, time, layout and gate counts as preregistered conditions.

## 03C: independent closure diagnostics

The exact six-face curl satisfies bᵀF=0 modulo 2π, b=(-1,1,1,-1,-1,1). Fitting five chords forces this equality. Therefore the reported zero residual is not an independent test.

Intact-cube exploratory extension: add a sixth local parameter q by shifting the probe offset of face f by b_f q/6. This produces a family of setting-dependent Hamiltonians, not a static cube with inconsistent curvature. In a linearized calibrated response around the commanded field, let A be the 24-by-5 chord Jacobian and g the q derivative. With V = binomial diagonal plus the frozen shared response covariance, whiten the residuals using a Cholesky factor. Rank [A g]=6 certifies local separation of the extension from the five static chords. The one-parameter GLS score and uncertainty diagnose excess setting-dependent closure-like error, with model dependence and posthoc limitations explicitly recorded. A held-out offset/field study is needed for calibration.

A gauge transform puts the oriented flux on the local (1,3) edge. Verify this equivalence with the 8-site masked graph and check the spectator readout/no leakage in future compiled circuits.

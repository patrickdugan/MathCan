## Source and scope

Kretschmer, Qian, Tal, arXiv:2411.02554v1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02554v1, printed pp. 5–6 (Lemma 3 and trapdoor hybrid) and p. 9 (future directions). The source's product-block lemma gives a tail bound for AC0 Boolean disagreement under one independently resampled uniformly chosen block; it is not a bound for arbitrary correlated blocks. The original trapdoor proof first reprograms a whole (G,I) block and removes the target public parameter from the range of G before applying product-row reasoning to F. A uniform target row and an independently sampled public parameter have different roles. We cannot replace that consistency hybrid by the lemma below. In particular the candidate truncation of F in QBTC-04 still has no security reduction against arbitrary collision finders.

## Written theorem: multirow empirical domination

Let N>k>=1, M>=2, X_1,...,X_N iid uniform in [M], and Z_i sampled independently conditional on X from arbitrary finite encoding laws D_(X_i). Write D=(1/M) sum_v D_v; unconditionally Z is distributed as D^N. Independently of (X,Z), choose a uniform k-subset I of destination rows. In independent replacement redraw every destination from D. In empirical replacement choose k source indices independently with replacement among untouched rows, copy their labels, and independently redraw the destination encodings from the corresponding D_v. In particular the replacement does not copy physical source bits. Denote by S_ind,k and S_copy,k the expected cost g(Z,Z') in [0,B] in these experiments; g may depend on the full old and new table and destination set, but not on extra source-index side information.

Fix X,Z,I and write n_v for the untouched-label counts. The destination laws under empirical replacement are iid from D_hat=sum_v n_v D_v/(N-k), conditional on this context. On good event max_v n_v <= (1+delta)(N-k)/M, D_hat <= (1+delta)D pointwise. Taking products and weighting by nonnegative g yields conditional cost domination by (1+delta)^k. Outside the event bound the cost by B. For each fixed I, the untouched labels remain iid uniform; multiplicative Chernoff and the union bound give, for 0<delta<=1,
S_copy,k <= (1+delta)^k S_ind,k + B M exp(-delta^2 (N-k)/(3M)).
No restriction on D_v beyond being probability laws is needed. The additive term can be capped by B. The finite Lean targets capture weighted mixture domination, product domination and exceptional mass integration; the probabilistic identification and Chernoff bound are written arguments, not part of the kernel receipt.

## Independent multiresampling and KQT composition

For Boolean f, g=1[f(Z)!=f(Z')], replace the k uniformly chosen distinct indices in random order. Every intermediate table has product law D^N conditional on the chosen indices because fresh replacements are independent. The marginal index at each step is uniform, and the union bound over steps gives S_ind,k <= k S_ind,1. Consequently,
S_copy,k <= k(1+delta)^k S_ind,1 + M exp(-delta^2(N-k)/(3M)).
Take delta=1/k, so the multiplicative coefficient is at most e k.

For finite-dimensional real or complex Euclidean observers and squared distance cost, the same independent k-row bound follows from the orthogonal product ANOVA decomposition. Write f=sum_{A subset [N]} f_A, with each nonempty component mean zero in every coordinate in A and dependent only on A. For fixed I, replacing coordinates I by independent draws gives E||f(Z')-f(Z)||^2=2 sum_{A:A intersects I} ||f_A||_L2^2. Indeed unchanged components coincide and cross terms of changed components vanish by conditioning on a coordinate on which they have mean zero; for each changed component the mixed old/new inner product also vanishes. Averaging over uniform I, the hit probability is <=k|A|/N by a union bound. The one-row energy weights are exactly |A|/N. This yields S_ind,k<=k S_ind,1. For bounded Euclidean costs B must bound all possible squared differences; normalize before using B=1. This ANOVA argument is a written lemma, not a VPVNP-02J rank-increment corollary or new formal certificate.

For KQT Lemma 3, set p=N^(-1/2) and use S_ind,1<=p+Pr[conditional disagreement>=p]. The published tail (4N/p) times 2^(-pN/O(log s)^(d-1)) is negligible in lambda when N=2^(2lambda), fixed AC0 depth d, and log s polynomial in lambda. With M=2^lambda and k polynomial in lambda (k<N), the second term M exp(-(N-k)/(3 k^2 M)) is negligible too. Thus this RESTRICTED empirical-label replacement family has negligible expected Boolean sensitivity for such circuits, using the original KQT theorem. Markov also gives a tail bound for conditional sensitivity at any inverse-polynomial threshold. This is a conditional transfer from an existing theorem; it makes no claim of uniform AC0 sensitivity under all correlated updates, or BQP^PH query security under a reprogrammed full oracle. The updated table itself can be correlated even though the source table was initially product distributed.

## Negative controls and extraction obstacle

1. Adaptive source selection from a balanced two-label pool can force label 1 with probability 1, versus 1/2 under independent replacement. Good empirical counts alone do not control adaptive source selection.
2. Reusing a single sampled source for both destination labels gives equality probability 1 vs 1/M under independent replacements, even if the source histogram is exactly uniform. For k copies of a common label, the all-equal event has likelihood ratio M^(k-1). This differs from sampling source indices independently with replacement.
3. Copying actual encoded row bits rather than sampling from D_v can make equality to a fixed source encoding certain when independent conditional draws match with probability only 1/8 (or arbitrarily smaller in larger spaces). Good label counts do not repair this.
4. Even successful indistinguishability of the allowed row changes does not prove collision resistance: QBTC-04's planted-pair recovery identity gives a severe extraction loss. Resampling the trapdoor inversion table and keeping the PH overlay coherent also remain open.

## Exact pilot

qbtc05_check.mjs enumerates independent initial rows over four encoded states, with integer marginal masses (3,1,1,3)/8 and distinct conditional encoding distributions (3,1)/4 and (1,3)/4 for the two labels. N=3,4,5; k=1,2. For all destination sets and initial tables, enumerate all new encoded states, compute empirical product weights and independently sum over actual source index tuples. Check normalization and pointwise probability domination on each good context. Evaluate three Boolean observables (noise OR, label parity, all labels equal) and one weighted real Euclidean observer. Exact integer cost comparisons use distinct delta=1/4,1/2,1, giving 72 aggregate exceptional-mass and good-context comparisons. Also verify k=2 independent cost <=2 times k=1 for all observers/dimensions. Local execution: 18,304 contexts, 216,832 conditional outcomes, all checks passed; three finite negative controls measured from explicit finite sample spaces. Example N=5,k=2, all-labels-equal disagreement: independent 3/32, empirical 3/16. This difference is compatible with the transfer bound and confirms that the two laws are not identical. The CI independently checks the binomial bad-event probabilities and exact receipt arithmetic.

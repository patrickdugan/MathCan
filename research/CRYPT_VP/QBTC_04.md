## Source and candidate

Kretschmer, Qian, Tal, arXiv:2411.02554v1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02554v1, printed p. 9 (Future Directions), explicitly leaves quantum-computable collision resistance relative to P=NP open and calls for sensitivity bounds for correlated row updates. The trapdoor construction on pp. 21–22 encodes a random F with 3n-bit parameter, n-bit input, 6n-bit output, and a correlated inversion oracle I. A candidate under their very same oracle is H_P(x)=first lambda bits of F(P,x), n=2lambda, with honestly sampled public P. The output is compressing, ideal H_P is a uniform random table conditional on P and the independent choice of G, and the evaluation guarantee transfers by truncation. Its collision resistance under access to the full KQT oracle (including I and PH overlay) has NOT been proved. Access to a standalone ideal random function is a different game. Distinct domains should be encoded injectively into disjoint input regions or independently sampled parameters and audited; merely labeling a digest after evaluation does not bind its domain. General one-wayness does not imply arbitrary-collision resistance. See also Cakan et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05055, for related fully black-box quantum separation results; their oracle model is distinct.

Distinguish collision resistance (adversary chooses both messages after seeing public parameter), second-preimage resistance for a specified target, and target collision resistance (commits target before independent random hash key). A permissionless adversarial transaction builder can select both messages after fixed network parameters; target-before-key security alone does not generally cover the ledger. Keyless fixed-oracle collision resistance must specify uniform/advice model: nonuniform oracle-dependent advice can hardcode one collision per length for any compressing function. A public keyed setup and adaptive/multi-target security are separate requirements.

## Written lemma 1: planted pair is not a useful generic collision target

Let H:[N]->[M] be uniformly sampled, N>=2, M>=2. K=binom(N,2). C(H)=number of unordered colliding distinct pairs. Then
E C=K/M=mu, Var C=K(1/M)(1-1/M).
Distinct pair indicators are independent in pairs, even when they share one vertex, since the probability of three equal independent uniform labels is 1/M^2.

Plant a hidden uniform unordered pair e by sampling H conditional on H colliding on e (equivalently choose endpoints, overwrite one label with the other's). Write U for uniform table law and V for the marginal planted law. For each h,
V(h)=U(h)*C(h)/mu.
Conditional on h in the planted distribution, e is uniform among its C(h) colliding pairs. Therefore
TV(V,U)=E_U |C-mu|/(2mu) <= sqrt(Var C)/(2mu)=sqrt((M-1)/(4K)).
For ANY randomized collision selector A that depends only on h and independent randomness (or side information sampled identically conditional on h, independent of e), we have
Pr_V[A(h)=e] = Pr_U[A(h) is a valid collision]/mu.
Proof: sum V(h) times conditional selection probability divided by C(h) on positive-C tables; C cancels. If N>M, every table has a collision and even an unbounded always-successful selector has planted-pair recovery probability exactly 1/mu=M/K. This is an information-theoretic obstruction to the naive reduction that asks an arbitrary collision finder to identify the one hidden planted pair. It does not bound arbitrary collision finding itself.

For N=2^(2lambda), M=2^lambda, the two table laws have exponentially small total variation, but a generic collision selector returns the planted pair with probability at most about 2^(1-3lambda). Removing all collisions by resampling is impossible for N>M. Independently replacing one uniformly selected row by a fresh label destroys a previously selected collision with probability exactly (2/N)(1-1/M); the witness survives almost all such changes. This calculation assumes the chosen pair was fixed before the independent row/replacement draw. These observations explain why single-target one-wayness resampling cannot be copied verbatim into an arbitrary-collision proof.

## Written lemma 2: empirical domination for one copied label

There is a useful narrower sensitivity transfer that does not require global pair planting recovery. Let X=(X_1,...,X_N) be iid uniform labels in [M]. Conditional on X, sample encoded rows Z_i independently from arbitrary distributions D_(X_i). A cost g(Z,Z') in [0,1] can be any measurable observer change (e.g. a Boolean disagreement or bounded normalized squared Euclidean difference). Let I be uniform row independent of X,Z. Independent replacement experiment: redraw Z_I from mixture D=(1/M)sum_v D_v. Copied-label experiment: choose J uniformly among rows other than I and redraw Z_I independently from D_(X_J), leaving all other rows unchanged. Crucially copy the LABEL and freshly sample encoding; do not copy the physical row or give J to the observer.

For a fixed realization X,Z,I=i, let c_v count labels among the N-1 other rows. The replacement measure in copied-label experiment is sum_v c_v D_v/(N-1). On the event max_v c_v <= (1+delta)(N-1)/M, this measure is dominated by (1+delta)D, for every measurable event. Thus for 0<delta<=1,
E[g]_(copy) <= (1+delta) E[g]_(independent) + M exp(-delta^2 (N-1)/(3M)).
Proof: integrate the pointwise inequality for nonnegative g over X,Z,I; on the bad event bound g by 1. Conditional on I=i, each c_v is Bin(N-1,1/M), so multiplicative Chernoff and a union bound give the displayed tail. This is a cost comparison, not a total-variation claim for the joint original/new states, nor a bound for a fully adaptive chosen source J. It extends directly to a cost upper bounded by B by multiplying the tail term by B.

For N=2^(2lambda), M=2^lambda, fixed delta>0, the tail is superexponentially small in lambda. In an independent Forrelation row encoding of a compressing random table, any bound on the expected independent-row observer cost transfers to this one copied-label experiment. The same derivation works for any finite bounded observer, so VPVNP-style squared Euclidean observables fit after normalization. This does NOT extend the certified rank-one/full-rank theorem to arbitrary product distributions. It also does NOT solve KQT's full challenge: the inversion table I is correlated with F, the oracle collapse layer must be consistent, and a collision finder need not locate a randomly planted pair (lemma 1). The proof must specify which oracle rows form one independent block and how inverse consistency changes under reprogramming; a full coupling with the collapse overlay and a reduction for an arbitrary returned witness remain outstanding.

## Conditional ledger commitment and no-compression baseline

If a quantum-computable keyed CR compression function with uniform pointwise evaluation gap and suitable domain separation is established, a fixed-depth Merkle tree of canonical leaf/node encodings can reduce two different openings of one root to a collision by descending from the equal root to a first differing child tuple. Use fixed depth/shape, explicit tags and lengths, a valid key generation model, and the same key/parameters for verification. A proof of collision resistance for the compression primitive, reproducible quantum verification with finite reliability bounds, and consensus assumptions remain required. This is the usual binding argument conditional on CR, not an implementation of such a hash.

An alternative test baseline avoids digest compression altogether. For a bounded ledger history, store canonical complete history prefix and explicit block/transaction/output position as the reference, retain the complete spending authorization key, and sign raw bounded transaction context using QBTC-03 one-time authorization. Position alone aliases across forks. Full prefix distinguishes different histories by injective canonical encoding, assuming it contains all relevant fields. A shared prefix can still permit same-network fork replay; no replay protection against identical context is claimed. Full prefix material grows with history, so this baseline cannot replace a succinct Bitcoin txid or UTXO accumulator and does not establish an efficient indefinitely growing ledger (polynomial bounds and key sizes must be fixed). It assumes an external agreed ledger history/order, gives no proof of work, fork choice, or consensus security. Use it to isolate authorization from hash-binding assumptions before introducing compression. The finite script uses restricted arrays of canonical strings/integers, not a Bitcoin serialization or actual ledger implementation.

## Exact pilot

qbtc04_check.mjs enumerates every function for N in {4,6,8}, M in {2,3,4}: 9 cases, 77,595 tables, exact integer pair-count histogram and independent occupancy checks. It checks mean, variance, size-biased planted mass, total-variation bound, planted recovery rate, all single-row replacement choices for the selected first collision, and injective-table census. Five finite raw-reference controls distinguish position aliasing, full prefixes, output index, network/genesis context and identical-prefix replay. Example N=8,M=4: TV=975/8192, plant recovery=1/7, destroy selected pair=3/16; arbitrary collision exists in every table. The fixture verifies lemma 1 only; lemma 2 is a written inequality and requires its own registered validation/Lean gate. Initial aggregate expected table count typo was corrected from 78,460 to the exact sum 77,595 before committing; no enumerated case was dropped. Independent CI pending.

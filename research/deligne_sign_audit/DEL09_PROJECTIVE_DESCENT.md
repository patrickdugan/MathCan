**Source:** [Hexagon 2611.00789v1, Sections 6–7, especially Lemma 8](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00789v1), citing [Katz, Affine cohomological transforms, perversity, and monodromy, Appendix A.3–A.5, pp. 219–221](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/ACT.pdf).

## 1. The graph into the projective chart is quasi-finite

Let U be a smooth affine F_q-scheme of pure dimension d >= 1. For each k, write X=U^k with D=kd. Choose an affine closed immersion z^k:X→A^N, and append the fixed regular function

\[
B(u_1,\ldots,u_k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b(\pi(u_j),\pi(u_{j+1})).
\]

The new map \(\Gamma=(z^k,B):X\to\mathbf A^{N+1}\) is again a closed immersion: if the original coordinates generate \(\mathcal O(X)\) as an algebra, adjoining an extra coordinate does not destroy generation. DEL-08 proves that statement as an actual affine-scheme closed immersion, by checking that an evaluation map on the extended polynomial ring is surjective.

Include \(\mathbf A^{N+1}\) in \(\mathbf P^{N+1}\) as its **standard affine chart**. The chart is an open immersion, and it is quasi-compact because it is a principal projective open. In Mathlib v4.35, the following instance chain is already available:

\[
\mathrm{IsClosedImmersion}
\implies\mathrm{IsImmersion}
\implies\mathrm{LocallyQuasiFinite},
\]

and locally quasi-finite morphisms are stable under composition. An immersion is also locally of finite type. Closed immersions are quasi-compact, and the composition of quasi-compact morphisms is quasi-compact.

DEL-09's ProjectiveQuasiFinite.lean applies that chain to the **actual DEL-08 graph map**, followed by an arbitrary open-immersion chart. When the chart is additionally assumed quasi-compact, the composite map is quasi-compact and its fibers over all scheme points are finite.

This supplies the correct scheme-theoretic finiteness condition to apply **Katz Appendix A.5**, provided the chart is the actual standard affine chart in projective space.

**Formal boundary:** DEL-09 supplies the *chart morphism and its open-immersion/quasi-compactness properties as named hypotheses*. It does not construct the canonical A^N→P^N chart map from a projective-space functor or prove that it identifies the fixed-coordinate projective chart. It proves the corresponding consequence for every such chart. Constructing that canonical map is an explicit independent formalization target.

## 2. The descent argument now holds for arbitrary finite coordinate tuples

Katz A.5 and Bertini produce a dense open in a projective hyperplane parameter space over the algebraic closure \(\overline{\mathbf F}_q\). The intersection with the fixed-coefficient chart is geometrically nonempty. Once a point has been selected, write its finite collection of chart coordinates as \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_N\in\overline{\mathbf F}_q\).

Each \(\alpha_i\) is algebraic over \(\mathbf F_q\). Thus the intermediate field

\[
E=\mathbf F_q(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_N)
\]

is finite-dimensional, hence a **finite field extension** of \(\mathbf F_q\), and all chosen coefficients belong to E.

FiniteParameterDescent.lean proves this for an arbitrary finite coefficient set and for an indexed tuple, using the real Mathlib intermediate field generated by the chosen elements. It does so by combining:

- the algebraicity of intermediate fields of an algebraic extension;
- finite generation of an intermediate field obtained by adjoining finitely many elements;
- the theorem that an algebraic and essentially finite-type field extension is module-finite;
- the finiteness of a finitely generated module over a finite field.

The statement does **not** require the full ambient algebraic closure to be finite-dimensional. It also does not assume that the good locus has a point over the original base field. The extension degree e_k may vary with k, as permitted by DEL-05.

This is an actual all-finite-tuples field-theoretic descent theorem, distinct from the finite model oracles in DEL-08.

The assembled Lean theorem exists_good_parameter_over_finite_extension now also composes DEL-08's dense-open fixed-chart selection with that field-theoretic result. With named Bertini, weak-Lefschetz and chart subsets of an affine parameter space, their prescribed density/openness assumptions give a selected point; the theorem constructs the finite intermediate field of its coordinates and proves their membership. Its topological assumptions are generic over the coordinate-parameter space; an actual Zariski-topology/projective-chart implementation remains future geometric work.

**Formal boundary:** finding the geometric point in the intersection of the Bertini/Katz opens is still a geometric premise. The result says that *any finitely many parameters selected in the algebraic closure* have a common finite field of definition. To apply it to a scheme, the chosen parameter point must come from such a finite tuple and the defining conditions must descend from the algebraic closure.

## 3. Exact finite-field subfield checks

DEL-09's exact arithmetic oracle independently verifies minimal fields of definition in F4, F8, F9, F16, F25, F27.

For coefficients \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\in\mathbf F_{p^m}\), the unique subfield of order \(p^e\) (for each divisor e of m) is cut out by

\[
x^{p^e}=x.
\]

Consequently the least common subfield degree is the smallest divisor e of m for which every a_i satisfies this equation. The finite-field oracle checks the complete fixed field has precisely \(p^e\) elements, and verifies every coefficient's membership. In F16 it distinguishes the proper F4 subfield from coefficients that generate the entire F16.

A second exact oracle tests the principal open

\[
D(X^p-X)\subset\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf F_p}.
\]

It contains no F_p-rational point, while F_(p²) contains exactly \(p²-p\) points of this open. This is an adversarial test for a mistaken assumption that Katz's generic hyperplane parameters can always be chosen over the ground field.

## 4. Projective charts, finite fibers, and geometric mutant cases

On the standard projective affine chart, the graph of an element x with an arbitrary quadratic regular function B(x) is represented by

\[
[1:x:B(x)].
\]

The exact oracle checks that preserving the x coordinate gives singleton fibers on the finite sets of rational points. Dropping that coordinate yields the mutant \([1:x^p-x:0]\), which collapses every F_p-rational x to the same point. This finite-set diagnostic supports the embedding acceptance rule, while the genuine closed-immersion result comes from the Lean theorem on surjective coordinate rings.

The 96-case corpus separates 24 common-subfield certificates, 18 finite-extension principal-open cases, 16 projective graph tests, 12 collapsed-coordinate mutants, 12 points at projective infinity, eight zero normalization coefficients, and six ambient-to-subvariety tangency mutants.

A finite-point injectivity check is not a proof of a scheme-theoretic closed immersion. The latter is supplied by the DEL-08 and DEL-09 Lean statements.

## 5. Remaining geometry, and what DEL-09 certifies

The actual point in the generic good locus still depends on the **Bertini smoothness open** and **Katz weak Lefschetz open** being nonempty and dense. Katz's Corollary A.5 assumes the quasi-finite map is separated of finite type and the coefficient sheaf is perverse. DEL-09's immersion results address the quasi-finite and locally-finite-type portions. The ambient morphism must remain separated and of finite type, and the sheaf's perversity and generic base-change assumptions must be proved or supplied.

The missing geometric proof steps are:

1. Construct the standard projective-space affine-chart map in Mathlib, including its open immersion and quasi-compactness, and identify it with the chosen coordinate chart.
2. Construct the actual smooth and weak-Lefschetz dense-open subschemes in the hyperplane parameter scheme, with the correct base field and connected-component quantifiers. DEL-08 currently uses their density and openness as hypotheses.
3. Establish the existence of a geometric point in their intersection and the descent of this **parameter scheme point** from the algebraic closure to the finite intermediate field generated by its chart coordinates.
4. Apply Katz A.3–A.5 to the specific quasi-finite map with the shifted lisse perverse sheaf and verify the resulting actual restriction maps, then use DEL-07's Poincaré/Gysin transfer.
5. Combine the resulting Frobenius-equivariant Gysin kernel with DEL-06's tensor eigenvectors, DEL-05's power limit, and DEL-02's Fourier character-sum estimate.

The reference paper is explicitly AI-generated and reported as having had no human proof checking at publication. This MathCan work checks its formal algebraic dependencies and the shape of the geometric argument. It has **not** established Deligne's theorem in Lean.

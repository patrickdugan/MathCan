## Sharp algebraic criterion

Consider

\[
F(X,Y)=XY+aX+bY+c\quad\text{in }K[X,Y]
\]

over any field K. Let J=(F,∂F/∂X,∂F/∂Y) be the ideal in the actual multivariate polynomial ring generated by F and both Mathlib partial derivatives.

DEL-13 proved

\[
F-(\partial_YF)(\partial_XF)=c-ab.
\]

If c-ab is nonzero, multiplying by its inverse gives 1 in J, so J=(1).

DEL-14 proves the converse. If c=ab, set X=-b,Y=-a. The polynomial and both actual partial derivatives vanish under the evaluation ring homomorphism

\[
\operatorname{ev}_{-b,-a}:K[X,Y]\to K.
\]

Thus J is contained in the evaluation kernel. That kernel is proper because evaluation sends the constant polynomial 1 to 1. Hence J is proper.

Together these give

\[
J=(1)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c-ab\ne0.
\]

The complementary proper-ideal criterion is exactly c=ab.

## Lean implementation

The file experiments/deligne_mathlib/Deligne04/SharpJacobianIdeal.lean proves seven statements:

- Evaluation of F at (-b,-a) gives zero on the discriminant.
- Both actual polynomial partial derivatives vanish at that point.
- The full Jacobian ideal is contained in the kernel of the evaluation ring homomorphism.
- The ideal is proper when c=ab.
- The ideal equals top precisely when c-ab is nonzero.
- The complementary properness criterion is the discriminant equation.

The proof uses actual Mathlib Ideal.span, RingHom.ker, and MvPolynomial.eval.

## Independent exact regression

The 72-case oracle includes 24 explicit unit ideals, 24 proper ideals, 12 singular evaluations away from the origin and 12 incorrect-sign discriminant mutants. Twelve tests compare the sharp equivalence over F2, F3, F4, F5, F8, F9, F16, F25 and F27.

The singular evaluation proves ideal properness as a ring-homomorphism statement and does not infer it from finite-point enumeration. A separate test keeps nonzero formal polynomials distinct from polynomial functions that vanish on all finite rational points.

## Geometric boundary

This is a sharp **global Jacobian ideal condition for the affine conic model**. A scheme-theoretic smoothness equivalence for Spec(K[X,Y]/F) additionally invokes the affine hypersurface Jacobian criterion and finite-presentation/Kaehler differential machinery. That can be a further MathCan formal target.

The original proposed proof concerns an arbitrary embedded variety U^k. DEL-14 does not construct its relative singular incidence, Bertini smoothness open, or Katz weak Lefschetz open; these are separate geometric prerequisites. The Hexagon note identifies itself as AI-authored without human verification.

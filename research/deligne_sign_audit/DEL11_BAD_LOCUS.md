## The concrete critical-point incidence

Over any field K and for any n, consider the affine family

\[
H_{a,b,c}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb A^{2n}:
\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i y_i+a_i x_i+b_i y_i)+c=0\}.
\]

The gradient blocks are \(\partial H/\partial x_i=y_i+a_i\) and \(\partial H/\partial y_i=x_i+b_i\). The only simultaneous critical point is \((x,y)=(-b,-a)\). Its phase value equals \(\Delta(a,b,c)=c-\sum_i a_i b_i\). Thus critical-point incidence projects exactly onto the parameter hypersurface \(\Delta=0\).

A characteristic-free completion of the square proves:

\[
\sum_i(x_i y_i+a_i x_i+b_i y_i)+c
 =\sum_i(x_i+b_i)(y_i+a_i)+\Delta(a,b,c).
\]

This uses no division by 2 and works in characteristic two.

## Lean statements

BilinearBadLocus.lean proves the complete-square identity and critical-point equivalence over arbitrary commutative rings. It creates the actual multivariate parameter polynomial Delta and proves its evaluation formula. It identifies the existential critical-point set with Mathlib's polynomial zero locus of the ideal generated by Delta. The point a=b=0,c=1 proves Delta is a nonzero polynomial.

Over a field, the Jacobian criterion says that a hypersurface with no simultaneous zero of its partial derivatives on its level set is smooth or empty. The Lean theorem proves the nonvanishing-gradient algebra; the scheme-theoretic Jacobian criterion remains an external geometric theorem.

The final theorem invokes DEL-10: given a supplied Katz-good principal-open polynomial L and the coordinatewise degree budget

\[
\deg_{t_i}\Delta+\deg_{t_i}L<|K|
\]

there is an actual rational parameter with nonzero Delta and L.

For F_q, each pair a,b has precisely one singular constant c, so the number of bad parameters is q^(2n) and the number of good parameters is q^(2n)(q-1). The independent finite-field oracle verifies this exact identity.

## General ideal-to-principal-open extraction

IncidenceIdeal.lean treats a polynomial ideal I describing a bad locus in affine parameter space over a field K. If a selected parameter t lies outside its zero locus, some P in I has P(t) nonzero. The principal open D(P) contains t and avoids every bad point known to lie in the zero locus of I.

A second theorem extracts two such nonzero polynomials from two bad-locus ideals simultaneously. These can serve as Bertini and Katz principal-open certificates, given explicit assumptions that the ideals cover their respective bad loci and share a point outside both zero loci.

**Geometric boundary:** These conditional theorems do not construct the general Bertini tangent-incidence ideal, prove the projection of the critical incidence has proper Zariski closure, or produce quantitative degree bounds. Those are actual geometric premises.

The source's variety is an arbitrary smooth affine embedded U^k with a fixed bilinear last coordinate. A smooth ambient hypersurface can become singular on an embedded subvariety, as shown by DEL-08's tangent-line counterexample. The explicit Delta=c-sum(a_i b_i) only describes the unconstrained affine bipartite model, and it does not automatically characterize singular sections of general U^k.

## DEL-12 geometric program

To generalize from the explicit model to the actual embedded U^k in Lemma 8, the next work must present U^k by an affine ideal, build the universal hyperplane family with its fixed bilinear coefficient, and define the relative singular incidence via Jacobian rank or Fitting ideals of relative differentials. Proving its projected bad locus has proper closure requires the tangent-incidence dimension estimate. A nonzero polynomial can then be extracted from a certified ideal of that closure, and its degree may be bounded if an effective extension cutoff is wanted.

The weak-Lefschetz good-open condition requires Katz's perverse sheaf and generic-base-change input. Its bad-parameter polynomial is not supplied by the explicit affine discriminant. Combining those true geometric polynomials with DEL-10 and DEL-09 will connect the chosen good hyperplanes to the finite-extension spectral argument.

# RHD-08 | decomposition groups and quadratic prime fibers

Source baseline: `leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis`, upstream commit `f150e02623b1e2bbe1f1da8eab5ffee5e930a6a3`. Builds on RHD-07 (`RHD07_FIELDOK_NONVACUITY.md`). This note is a **mathematical inference about cyclotomic prime splitting**, with finite-group certificates and explicit outstanding formalization steps.

## General splitting theorem

Let `E=Q(zeta_n)`, `G=Gal(E/Q)=(Z/nZ)^×`, and suppose `ell` is a rational prime coprime to `n`. The decomposition subgroup at any prime of E over `ell` is `D=<ell mod n>`; the residue degree is `|D|=ord_n(ell)`. Its primes correspond to cosets `G/D`.

Let `K=Q(x)` be an imaginary quadratic subfield generated by `x²=-d` and let `H=Gal(E/K)`. The prime contraction map is `G/D → G/(HD)`, where `HD` is the subgroup generated by H and D. Its fiber size is

```
|HD|/|D| = |H|/|H ∩ D|.
```

Therefore two distinct prime ideals of E over ell contract to the same prime of K precisely when `|H| > |H∩D|`. If the local square condition `SqAt d ell` holds and `ell` is odd and unramified, then ell splits in K, so `D ⊆ H`. In that case the fiber size simplifies to `|H|/|D|`. A pair exists iff the order of ell modulo n is *strictly less* than `|H|`.

The `OneK x w w'` proposition uses membership of integral elements in the field `Q(x)`. In a Galois cyclotomic field this is exactly equality of contractions of the two prime ideals to that quadratic subfield. A finite-group coset certificate represents the right arithmetic fact. Constructing `HeightOneSpectrum(O_E)` and checking `OneK` in Lean remains a separate acceptance gate.

## Conductor-level theorem

Every decomposition group is cyclic. If `H` is **noncyclic**, the decomposition subgroup is always proper whenever ell splits in K. Thus every unramified split ell has a distinct pair of primes of E contracting to the same prime of K. If H is cyclic, Dirichlet's theorem supplies infinitely many unramified rational primes whose residue modulo n is a generator of H. At those primes `D=H` and the contraction fiber has size one.

In a finite abelian group, the largest cyclic subgroup has order equal to the exponent. Hence the **minimum possible fiber size** over ell splitting in K is

```
min |H|/ord_n(ell) = |H|/exponent(H).
```

The proof uses Dirichlet to realize any generator's coprime residue class as a rational prime. In particular, the field-level guarantee holds for all such ell iff H is noncyclic.

## Satisfying the other FieldOK arithmetic requirements

The RHD-07 source check found `FieldOK E ell d` requires `ImQ E d`, `SqAt d ell`, an even real-subfield degree, and split or unramified behavior at every place above ell.

For `n` divisible by 4, take `d=1`, `K=Q(i)`, and `H={g∈G : g ≡ 1 (mod 4)}`. For n divisible by 3, take `d=3`, `K=Q(sqrt(-3))`, `H={g∈G : g ≡ 1 (mod 3)}`.

For the selected 14 conductors `n ∈ {12,15,20,21,24,28,30,36,40,44,45,48,60,84}`, the real-subfield degree `φ(n)/2` is even. Put `m = n/gcd(n,2)`. The relative polynomial of ζ over the maximal real subfield is `X²-(ζ+ζ^-1)X+1`, with derivative `ζ-ζ^-1 = ζ^-1(ζ²-1)`. For these conductors, m is not a prime power, hence `Φ_m(1)=1` and the derivative is a unit of O_E. Since `O_F[ζ]=O_E`, this proves that E/F is unramified at every finite place.

At ell coprime to n, `SqAt` then corresponds to `ell ≡ 1 (mod 4)` for d=1 or `ell ≡ 1 (mod 3)` for d=3. This forces `D⊆H`, so complex conjugation `-1∉D` and all primes above ell split in E/F. Thus the source's arithmetic FieldOK gate holds under the stated conditions.

## New concrete degree-two certificates

- `n=20, ell=29, d=1`: `G` has order 8, `H={1,9,13,17}`, `D={1,9}`. The prime fiber has size 2; the residue degree of each prime of E is 2. SymPy independently factors `Φ_20` into four irreducible quadratics modulo 29.
- `n=24, ell=5, d=1`: `G` has order 8, `H={1,5,13,17}`, `D={1,5}`. Again the prime fiber has size 2, although no prime of E over ell has residue degree 1.
- `n=21, ell=13, d=3`: `|H|=6`, `|D|=2`, and the prime fiber has size 3.

These demonstrate that RHD-07's completely split criterion is specific to its biquadratic field. A higher-degree CM field can supply distinct prime contractions even when the cyclotomic polynomial has only degree-two factors.

## Residue degree four

The same coset count gives an example with residue degree four in the abelian CM field $E=\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{16})^+(\sqrt{-547},\sqrt{-727})$. Take $K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{-547})$. The three independent factors of $\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbf{Q})$ have orders $4,2,2$, so $|H|=8$. The unramified prime $11$ splits in $K$ since $5^2\equiv-547\pmod{11}$. Its class modulo $16$ has order four up to sign ($11^2\equiv9$ and $11^4\equiv1\pmod{16}$), so the decomposition group $D$ has order four and lies in $H$. Each of the two primes of $K$ above $11$ therefore has $8/4=2$ primes of $E$ above it, each of residue degree four over $\mathbf{Q}$. [Crossley’s field and Frobenius calculation](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-short-shifts/blob/e1b53f66b171e1a2acb0561b910abd1215546379/PROOF.md#L29), [archived paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24410823).

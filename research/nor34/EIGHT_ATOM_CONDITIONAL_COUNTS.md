# NOR-34: full eight-pattern hypergeometric matching

The prospective [NOR-34 RRC25 experiment](https://github.com/MoralityLabAI/Compactification-Theory/blob/research/nor34-eight-atom-curvature-20261117/research/nor34_eight_atom_curvature/REGISTRATION.md) controls the three old public source-risk support memberships (tail, top magnitude, and lag) jointly, yielding eight disjoint public coordinate atoms.

A fixed attack quota vector q assigns q_j changed coordinates to the j-th atom. An honest target witness audits t_j of n_j positions in that atom. Exactly PRODUCT_j C(n_j−t_j,q_j) distinct attacked-position sets miss every inspected position, from PRODUCT_j C(n_j,q_j) possible equally likely attacks under the conditional quota-matched distribution.

The companion Lean 4.35 module implements the exact all-atoms enumerator with a recursive product of the NOR-23 native finite subsets, proves the exact binomial count identity, and proves its invariance under identical atom size and per-atom inspected-count vectors. A finite roster of distinct quota vectors preserves the equivalence, which is sufficient to standardize over the quota vector distribution induced by curvature-selected attacks. A deliberately small finite counterexample shows why a coarse old-union inspection count alone cannot deliver that equivalence.

This is a counting theorem over a fixed finite partition. The applied empirical question is whether the old three-feature membership atoms capture most of NOR-33's curvature-versus-random difference when the attacker transfers positions **within** those same atom categories. The theorem makes no statement about authenticity of external target-coefficient responses, cryptographic secrecy of actual audit coins, or transfer to unknown real-world source processes.

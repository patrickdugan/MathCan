## Counting theorem

Let N be odd composite, B>=2 integer, ell_1,...,ell_r distinct rational primes at most B, coprime to N, r>=1. Assume positive e_i with ell_i^e_i=1 mod N. Set Mstar=lcm_i(e_i).

For p|N prime, H_p is the subgroup of F_p^* generated by the ell_i. Since the group is cyclic, its subgroup order equals its exponent, and |H_p| divides Mstar.

Let k=floor(log_B(p-1)). All exponent vectors a in N^r with sum a_i<=k give integer products ell_i^a_i in [1,B^k] subset [1,p-1]. Unique factorization gives injectivity over the integers and the range gives injectivity modulo p. The products are elements of H_p. Stars and bars counts binom(r+k,k) such vectors. Therefore

    |H_p| >= binom(r+k,k)
    I_p=(p-1)/|H_p| <= floor((p-1)/binom(r+k,k)).

Including repeated primes and all degrees <=k improves the squarefree degree-k count and removes the restriction k<=r.

Let h=floor(log_B(N-1)). Uniform cap, independent of the prime factors:

    K_exact=max_{0<=j<=h} floor(min(N-1,B^(j+1)-1)/binom(r+j,j)).
    K_coarse=B if h=0, else B*ceil(B*h/r)^h.

Clamp either cap to N-1. Since binom(r+k,k)>= (r/k)^k for k>=1, and p-1<B^(k+1), I_p<B*(B*k/r)^k<=K_coarse. Here ceil(B*h/r)>=1 because r<=B and h>=1. For k=0, I_p<B. The same inequality bounds the terms of K_exact, so K_exact<=K_coarse after clamping.

For either cap K put S_K=lcm(1,...,K). Then I_p divides S_K and |H_p| divides Mstar. Thus the explicit integer

    M=2*Mstar*S_K

is even and p-1 divides M for every prime p|N. Repeated factors and prime powers cause no difficulty because the argument is over F_p. Checking the supplied relations is distinct from finding them.

## Asymptotic ledger

Let L=ln N. Conditionally assume r>=B/(C ln B) for a fixed C>0 and choose B near exp(sqrt(L ln L)). Then h<=L/ln B and ceil(B*h/r)<=1+C*L. Hence

    ln K_coarse <= ln B + h ln(1+C L) = O(sqrt(L ln L)).

Since S_K divides K!, ln S_K <= K ln K = exp(O(sqrt(L ln L))). This fits exp(L^0.65) eventually. Elementary iterative lcm computation has K iterations on integers of O(K ln K) bits, and hence exp(O(sqrt(L ln L))) total bit cost under polynomial-bit arithmetic.

For a sufficient conditional relation-size bound, suppose max_i ln(e_i) <= exp(o(L^0.65)). Since r<=B, ln Mstar <= sum_i ln(e_i) <= exp(o(L^0.65)). The resulting ln M is at most exp(o(L^0.65)), fitting the target output length. Computing Mstar from explicit exponents takes polynomial time in the aggregate bit length. The hypothesis on e_i is not verified for upstream algExp.

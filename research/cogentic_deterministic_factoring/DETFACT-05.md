## 1. Localized evaluation

Let N>2, f in Z[X] of positive degree, and integer m with f(m)=0 mod N. Evaluation followed by reduction defines a ring homomorphism Z[X]/(f) -> Z/NZ. No monicity assumption is needed for this statement. Let S be a multiplicatively closed set of polynomial residue classes whose images are units modulo N. The universal property of localization extends the homomorphism to S^(-1)(Z[X]/(f)), sending a/b to a(m)*b(m)^(-1) mod N. This does not extend to arbitrary elements of Q[X]/(f) or a whole number field: denominators divisible by factors of N cannot be inverted.

Concrete certificate for a rational equality: give integer polynomials f,a,b,c,d,h,q, with b,d,h nonzero, satisfying the exact polynomial identity

    h*(a*d-b*c)=f*q.

Check f(m)=0 mod N and gcd(b(m),N)=gcd(d(m),N)=gcd(h(m),N)=1. Evaluating the identity and cancelling h(m), b(m), d(m) proves

    a(m)/b(m) = c(m)/d(m) mod N.

A factor is already available whenever any required unit test gives a gcd strictly between 1 and N. If its gcd is N, the attempted denominator is zero modulo N and the current certificate is unresolved. The checker can return a factor from a different unit check if one is found; no division by a nonunit is permitted. In the concrete implementation the unit tests short-circuit, so a zero denominator can mask a later factor opportunity. That affects completeness only, not soundness.

The multiplier h may arise when clearing rational coefficients. If f is primitive and divides a*d-b*c over Q[X], Gauss's lemma supplies an integer quotient and h=1 can be used. For nonprimitive f, cancelling its content or leading coefficient without a valid unit or divisibility argument is unsafe. For example f=3(X+4), m=1, N=15 satisfies the root condition; 3(X+4)=f holds while X+4 evaluates to 5, not zero. Attempting to cancel 3 yields a proper gcd of 3 instead.

An optional denominator certificate is an exact polynomial Bezout identity A*b+B*f=h. If h(m) is a unit, then b(m) is a unit and its inverse equals A(m)/h(m) mod N. This is a sufficient certificate; gcd evaluation is the simplest direct verifier. Merely citing a resultant or norm requires a justified identity and an admissible denominator.

## 2. Two-field common evaluation

Let f_1,f_2 have the common root m modulo N, and choose localizations admitting the evaluation maps ev_1, ev_2. Suppose exact field certificates give, after transport,

    ell^E P_1 = s_1 mod N,
    P_2 = s_2 mod N,

where ell is a unit, E>0 and s_i in {+1,-1}. If ev_1(P_1)=ev_2(P_2) (the P_i above denote those images), then ell^E=s_1*s_2 mod N and ell^(2E)=1 mod N. The same modular nuisance product is essential. An identical symbolic label, abstract ideal exponent vector or equality of norms does not establish equality of evaluated elements. A direct checker verifies the two signed relations and shared image, then checks the final congruence independently.

If signed relations replace global signs by elements of any subgroup of known exponent t, then the analogous conclusion is ell^(tE)=1, provided the nuisance product agrees and the subgroup assumptions are checked. In particular square roots of unity modulo N have exponent dividing two even when not global signs. The implementation uses only explicit global signs.

## 3. Algebraic units are not automatically signs

Consider the real quadratic field generated by alpha with alpha^2-alpha-1=0. Its norm to Q is -1, and alpha*(alpha-1)=1. At m=8 and N=55 the defining polynomial evaluates to 55, so alpha maps to the unit 8. Its square maps to 9 modulo 55, not 1. Thus algebraic-unit status, even norm -1, cannot justify a sign relation.

For a unit-group relation in actual number fields one needs the precise algebraic argument eliminating the unit contribution, or verified modular information about its image. A sufficient abstract gate is a product identity with a verified image of finite exponent t; a norm alone is not such a gate. The Cogentic-specific derivation of that image and its sign/exponent remains unverified.

## 4. Bit-cost and discovery boundary

Checking an explicit certificate h*(ad-bc)=fq with exact integer polynomial arithmetic is polynomial in the represented degrees and coefficient bit lengths. Modular evaluation, gcd and modular exponentiation take polynomial bit work in explicit inputs (for modular exponentiation, the exponent's bit length). The polynomial check does not imply that a compact input exponent can be expanded into an explicit polynomial or integer of polynomial bit size. In the synthetic fixtures below, ell^E is explicitly expanded as a small integer; this is not a valid general complexity argument for the upstream algorithm.

For large exponents, use a bounded straight-line certificate with repeated squaring and locally verified reductions/identities, or prove a bound on the expanded representation. Each node must have an explicit bit-width bound, denominator validity and exact semantic connection to its parents; counting DAG nodes alone is insufficient. Nonmonic quotient reductions may require leading-coefficient inverses and hence extra localization checks. No efficient field relation discovery or algebraic-unit computation is implemented here.

Once a target exponent is positive and verified by modular powering, it can be passed to DETFACT-02. DETFACT-04 consumes already verified relation vectors; it cannot manufacture valid algebraic identities merely from integer matrix rank.

## 5. Exact replay

Run node localization_audit.mjs. All arithmetic is BigInt. The fixtures construct negative-discriminant integer quadratics with a common root modulo small odd composite N. Their leading coefficients vary, including nonmonic cases. Exact rational-equality fixtures use a=k*c+f*R, b=k*d+f*S and q=h*(R*d-S*c). A separate checker multiplies polynomials, verifies the exact identity, checks unit conditions, and evaluates both fractions.

Two-field fixtures use exhaustively enumerated signed modular orders to supply toy E, and then build compatible rational identities in the two fields. The order oracle is deliberate: it supplies known relations for transport testing and is not an efficient relation producer. The checker independently confirms both field identities, shared modular image, signs and final annihilation. The optional Bezout inverse fixture tests X*(aX+b)-f=-f(0).

Adversarial controls cover forged quotient, bad common root, zero clearing polynomial, invalid signs, wrong exponent, incompatible shared nuisance values, proper-factor gcd, zero denominator, masked equality through nonunit clearing, nonunit target and norm-one/sign confusion (the example has norm -1). The same audit function ran in the authoring JS runtime; Node was not separately executed. The replay is small, synthetic and does not establish coverage of any unknown prime-factor family.

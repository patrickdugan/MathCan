## The evaluation hyperplane

Let `S=G\{O}`, with `N` odd and prime, and use characteristic zero or a prime greater than three. The function `Phi(x)` vanishes simply at every point of `S` and has pole order `N-1` at `O`. Consequently `w=1/Phi(x)` has divisor `(N-1)[O]-sum(S)`.

For `h` in `L((N-2)O)`, the differential `h*w*omega` has no pole at `O`. Its only possible poles occur at `S`, where its residues are `lambda_Q*h(Q)`. The global residue theorem gives the one linear relation on evaluations. Evaluation is injective because a nonzero member would otherwise have at least `N-1` zeros and at most `N-2` poles. Riemann-Roch gives dimension `N-2`, which identifies the image with `lambda^perp`.

At the next pole budget, `L((N-1)O)` has the one-dimensional evaluation kernel generated by `Phi(x)`. Its evaluation image is the same hyperplane. For `D=floor(N/3)`, the pole budget `3D` equals one of these two values.

Polynomials of degree at most `D` on a short Weierstrass cubic span `L(3DO)`. One direct basis uses `x^a y^b` with `0<=a<=2` and `a+b<=D`. Reduction by the cubic equation spans the restrictions of all degree-`D` polynomials. The displayed monomials have distinct pole orders, namely every nongap at most `3D`; there are `3D` of them when `D>=1`.

For a basis evaluation matrix `V`, choose a left inverse `L`. If a symmetric target matrix `M` has columns in the image of `V`, then `VLM=M`; symmetry also gives `ML^T V^T=M`. Hence `M=V(LML^T)V^T`, which constructs a decoder in the two coordinate blocks. Finally, assigning `u` at the identity adds `u` times the negation permutation. Its action on the odd residue vector is `-lambda`, yielding the eigenvector equation used by the certificate.

## Tate chart coverage

Translate the marked point to the origin and its tangent to `y=0`. The resulting Weierstrass equation has `a4=a6=0`. Smoothness and a nonvertical tangent give `a3!=0`; a point of order greater than three is not a flex, giving `a2!=0`. The rational scaling factor `a3/a2` makes the two coefficients equal, so this step needs no extraction of field roots.

In Tate form, `x(2T)=b` and `x(3T)=c`. A zero `c` would identify `3T` with `T` or `-T`, while `b=c` would identify `2T` with `3T` or `-3T`. These contradict the stated prime order. Thus `r=b/c` and `t=c/(r-1)` are defined. The recurrence only divides by coordinates of multiples different from `T` and `-T`, so those divisions are valid. The factors removed from the order equations are nonzero by these same distinctness arguments.

## Structural residue identity

In short coordinates, the parameter `z=-x/y` at `O` has expansions `x=z^-2+O(z^2)` and `omega=(1+O(z^4))dz`. Translation uses the same local parameter at every group point. The local expansion at `Q` is

```
w(Q+z) = lambda_Q/z * (1+a_Q z+b_Q z^2+O(z^3)).
```

Evenness of `w` gives the opposite signs of `lambda_Q` and `a_Q` at `Q` and `-Q`, while `b_Q` is unchanged. In the differential `x(Q+Z)w(Z)omega`, the residue at `-Q` is `-lambda_Q*b_Q`. All its other residues sum to the row of `M_0 lambda`, with the omitted identity assigned zero. Therefore `u_Q=b_Q`.

For the derivation dual to `omega`, set `B=partial(partial(w)/w)`. Its double-pole terms are `+1/z^2` at the nonidentity group points and `-(N-1)/z^2` at the identity. There are no simple-pole terms. Subtracting `sum_{R in S}x(Z-R)-(N-1)x(Z)` removes every pole, so the difference is constant. The constant term at `Q` is `2b_Q-a_Q^2`. Translation permutes the group points; the regular terms on the right equal a constant independent of `Q` minus `N*x(Q)`. This proves

```
2(u_Q-u_T) = a_Q^2-a_T^2-N(x(Q)-x(T)).
```

The independent symbolic audit checks the reciprocal-series coefficients and the constant term of the second logarithmic derivative. The supplied certificate checks the resulting identity in each of its four integral algebras. These checks support the global argument at the stated points; the global residue theorem itself remains outside the Lean module.

## Matching characteristic

Pass to an algebraic closure and put `N=p>3`. For each `Q` in the subgroup, the function `R_Q(Z)=w(Z+Q)/w(Z)` has divisor `p([-Q]-[O])`. The local logarithmic derivative has no pole because every valuation is a multiple of `p`. Dividing by the nowhere vanishing invariant differential therefore gives a global regular function, hence a constant `kappa_Q`.

For nonzero `Q`, this constant is nonzero. Otherwise `dR_Q=0`, and over perfect constants `R_Q` is a pth power. A pth root would have divisor `[-Q]-[O]`. The genus-one principal-divisor criterion forces `Q=O`, a contradiction.

The multiplicative identity `R_(Q+R)(Z)=R_Q(Z+R)R_R(Z)` and translation invariance of the differential make `kappa` additive. At the identity, evenness of `w` gives `R_Q=constant*z^-p*(1+a_Q*z+...)`, so `kappa_Q=a_Q`. In particular, `a_(2Q)=2a_Q`. The structural identity now gives `2(u_(2Q)-u_Q)=3a_Q^2`, which is nonzero.

It declares no new axioms and contains no admitted goals. Its nine theorems use only the standard transitive axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, as listed individually in `proof_receipt.json`.

## Review of the new experiment models

The function-distance calculation in `robustness.py` uses the linear map

```
B[i,s] = lambda[(s-i) mod N],  i in {1,...,N-1}, s in {0,...,N-1}.
```

This follows by changing variables in the row of the translation matrix times the residue vector; `lambda[0]=0` supplies the omitted column. Every admissible function is in `ker B`. Since the residue vector sums to zero, the constant function belongs to this kernel. Every kernel vector has a unique expression `h+c` with `h(O)=0`. For fixed `h`, choosing the most frequent value of `f(P)-h(P)` on `S` minimizes the charged Hamming distance. Exhausting the normalized kernel therefore gives the exact minimum while permitting a free value at `O`.

The alternate support search solves the same syndrome equation. It always includes the column at `O`, and each charged support is a subset of `S`. Membership in their linear span is equivalent to the existence of a correction on that support. Exhausting smaller supports before the first successful one proves minimality. A successful support search can certify the distance without explicitly returning the correction coefficients; the receipt identifies the method used.

For the new symbolic certificates, let `Gamma` be the recomputed monic gcd of the marked-order polynomial and all obstruction numerators over one residue field. Write `d=max(1,deg Gamma)`. The replay checks that the discriminant raised to `d` has zero remainder modulo `Gamma`. This is equivalent to every root of `Gamma` over the algebraic closure being singular. Indeed, each root multiplicity of `Gamma` is at most its degree, so raising a polynomial vanishing at those roots to this power supplies every required multiplicity. Conversely, a root of a divisor of `Delta^d` must be a root of `Delta`. The constant polynomial `Gamma=1` represents an empty fibre and passes vacuously. This method handles repeated roots and inseparability without explicitly splitting the fibre. Testing `Gamma|Delta` alone was sufficient for exclusion but could leave a fully singular nonreduced fibre unresolved; the current power criterion closes that gap.

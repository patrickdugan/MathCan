# Translation-rank rigidity: proof audit and extensions

Evidence: written algebraic proofs and independent finite checks. The original Windows scripts were not available. This note does not establish a general circuit, randomized-communication or quantum-complexity lower bound.

## 1. Counting and the cyclic reducibility threshold

For a nonzero K-valued function on a finite abelian group, its distinct translates form a nonzero orbit in the r-dimensional translate module. Over F_Q this gives [G:Stab(f)] <= Q^r-1. Equality makes every nonzero vector cyclic, hence the commutative image of the group algebra acts through a field and the faithful quotient is cyclic of order Q^r-1. This verifies the equality argument, with the nonzero hypothesis retained.

For a cyclic group of order M prime to Q, a faithful translate module is multiplicity-free semisimple: it is a quotient of the regular group algebra. Let its irreducible dimensions be d_1,...,d_k, summing to r. Then M divides lcm_i(Q^{d_i}-1). For reducible modules (k>=2), the sharp maximum is

    T_Q(r) = (Q^a-1)(Q^(r-a)-1)/(Q-1),
    a = max{j : 1<=j<=floor(r/2), gcd(j,r)=1}.

The domain is r>=2 except (Q,r)=(2,2), where no such reducible rank-two module exists. Rank one is never reducible.

Proof: for two degrees use gcd(Q^a-1,Q^b-1)=Q^gcd(a,b)-1. A non-coprime pair has upper bound Q^r/(Q^2-1)<Q^(r-1)-1 for r>=4. Among coprime pairs the product increases up to the midpoint. For Q>=3 and k>=3, the lcm is bounded above by Q^r/(Q-1)^2<Q^(r-1)-1 (r>=3). For Q=2, except r=3,4,6,10, the best coprime split satisfies a>=floor(r/3)+1. This follows by taking a=(r-1)/2 when r is odd, r/2-1 when 4 divides r, and r/2-2 when r=2 mod 4. Thus with b=floor(r/3), every partition with k>=3 has a part <=b, giving lcm <=2^r(1-2^-b), whereas the optimal two-part product is strictly larger. At r=3,4,6,10, a part 1 reduces the lcm to a product of at least two smaller factors, strictly less than 2^(r-1)-1. If there is no part 1, the exceptional r=6 case has all parts 2, and the r=10 case either has a part 2 (upper bound 768<889) or partition (3,3,4) (lcm 105<889). For r=3,4 no partition with k>=3 lacks a part 1.

Attainment: let a,b=r-a be coprime. Use the cyclic subgroup generated by a pair of primitive elements of F_{Q^a} and F_{Q^b} acting by multiplication on their direct sum. The two simple modules are distinct when a!=b, and a trace functional nonzero on each summand yields a faithful translate module of rank r. For r=2, Q>=3 use the trivial and a primitive one-dimensional character. The ratio T_Q(r)/Q^r tends to 1/(Q-1); for Q=2 the resulting constant is an asymptotic supremum, not a nonvacuous condition M>=2^r.

## 2. Pole rigidity with separate degree and pole-count budgets

Let A be a smooth connected one-dimensional commutative algebraic group over F_q, G a finite subgroup of A(F_q) of order N, and K an extension of F_q. The degree d of h is the degree of its pole divisor on the smooth projective completion over an algebraic closure. Let t be the number of points of G at which h has a pole. Values assigned at these poles may be arbitrary. If h has a geometric pole inside A, then for any g differing from the evaluated f at <=k points,

    (rk_K(g)+1)(d+t+k) >= N.

Proof: pass to an algebraic closure without changing the matrix rank. On one G-orbit of an interior pole, choose the largest pole order m and use local parameters transported by G. The map taking order-m polar coefficients is equivariant and sends h to a nonzero vector supported at s<=d/m points. Its translate span has dimension >=N/s: the supports of a spanning family cover G. Hence the rational translate span W has dim>=N/s. If r=rk(g)>=dim W the result follows. Otherwise choose r+1 linearly independent rational translates of h. The corresponding translates of g satisfy a nonzero linear relation. The same combination w of rational translates is nonzero and has total pole degree <=(r+1)d. It vanishes at >=N-(r+1)(t+k) points, since each translated error or pole set has at most t+k points. The degree of its zero divisor is at most (r+1)d, proving the bound. This argument also works in wild characteristic. When t=0 it strengthens the stated k-error denominator to d+k, not 2d+k.

If h is regular on A, the polynomial (additive) or Laurent polynomial (torus after scalar extension) expansion gives rank <=d+1; on a complete elliptic curve a regular h is constant. The pole and regular cases give the gap with the corrected hypotheses.

For a separable isogeny with full geometric kernel H contained in G, deg(u∘phi)=|H|deg(u), and the rank is at most [G:H] if assignments at poles respect the quotient. In general deg(phi) need not equal the size of the rational kernel. For G cyclic multiplicative of order N, h(x)=1/(x^m-a), a!=0, a outside the image of x->x^m, the exact rank is N/gcd(m,N), via the geometric series in z=x^m. Rank N/d requires m|N and d=m; the unrestricted example is false. E.g. q=7, N=6, m=4, a=3 gives rank 3 rather than N/d=3/2.

### Degree-one Cauchy stability

For h(x)=1/(x-a) on an additive subgroup with a outside G, or on a multiplicative subgroup with a nonzero and outside G, the translation matrix is a Cauchy matrix up to nonzero column scaling. Every square minor is nonsingular. A k-point change affects at most k entries in each row. Choose l=floor(N/(k+1)) rows; at least N-lk>=l columns are untouched across those rows. A clean l-by-l Cauchy submatrix survives. Thus rk_k(f)>=floor(N/(k+1)), including exact full rank for k=0. No sharpness claim for every k.

## 3. Aperiodicity radius

Define w=|supp f|. If k>=w, rk_k(f)=0. For k<w all approximants are nonzero. Minimize [G:H] over all subgroups H (including the trivial subgroup) whose periodic-function space lies within distance k of f. Calling the minimum I_k, counting gives rk_k(f)>=ceil(log_Q(1+I_k)). For nontrivial G and k below the distance to any nontrivially periodic function, I_k=N. This resolves the rank-zero endpoint in the pasted Theorem 4.

For binary m-sequences of period 2^n-1 with n>=2, the autocorrelation argument gives rho>=2^(n-2). If n is even, 3 divides 2^n-1. Use the subgroup of order 3: on each nonconstant triple with a zeros and b ones, min(a,b)=ab/2. Thus the autocorrelation inequality is equality and rho=2^(n-2) for every even n. For prime period the only nontrivial stabilizer is the whole group, so rho is distance to a constant, 2^(n-1)-1. The exact rank remains n below rho. No assertion that the rank must drop at rho.

## 4. Spectral correction and explicit counterexample

On an elliptic curve, the cited Kohel–Shparlinski conductor argument needs the character after twisting to be geometrically nontrivial. A sufficient hypothesis is that the nontrivial additive phase has a pole of order prime to p; an unramified elliptic group character cannot cancel this ramification. The original reference is D. Kohel and I. Shparlinski, *On Exponential Sums and Group Generators for Elliptic Curves over Finite Fields*, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1: https://www.i2m.univ-amu.fr/perso/david.kohel/pub/character.pdf . The unrestricted claim fails even for a nonconstant h with no rational poles.

Take F_243=F_3[t]/(t^5-t+1), E:y^2=x^3-x, G=E(F_243). The polynomial is irreducible (no linear or quadratic divisor), N=244 (pair x with -x since -1 is nonsquare), and (t^3-t)^121=-1. Thus u=1/(x-t) has two geometric simple poles and no poles at rational points. h=u^3-u has degree 6. The trace to F_3 vanishes everywhere on G and F=exp(2 pi i Tr(h)/3)=1. Its rank is 1, while N^2/(4d^2q)=59536/34992>1. The verifier enumerates every point. On G_a, a linear phase is itself a group character, so cancellation by another character must separately be excluded. On tori, use the appropriate mixed-character conductor bound.

For any unit-modulus function F on any finite abelian G, write L=max_chi |sum_x F(x)chi(x)|. The translation matrix is a convolution matrix times a permutation and has operator norm L. Its squared Frobenius norm is N^2. If an arbitrary complex matrix differs entrywise by at most epsilon (0<=epsilon<=1), Eckart–Young gives rank >=(1-epsilon^2)N^2/L^2. Consequently the desired spectral theorem holds whenever L<=2d sqrt(q) has been established. The subgroup version follows by averaging extensions of characters over the annihilator of G in the full group dual.

Additional arbitrary k-error stability: choose l rows of the translation matrix of a k-point perturbation g. At least N-lk columns are entirely unchanged across those rows, if N-lk>=0. The clean submatrix has squared Frobenius norm >=l(N-lk) and operator norm <=L, so

    rk_k(F) >= max_{1<=l<=N, lk<=N} ceil(l(N-lk)/L^2).

This covers unbounded corrupted values without converting Hamming distance into an unjustified uniform error bound. It is a rank bound, not a randomized/quantum communication theorem.

## 5. Scope and remaining boundaries

Scalar extension preserves rank for the same matrix; changing the function by a trace need not preserve it. Minimum aperiodic rank equals the minimum number of group generators over a splitting field of characteristic prime to the group order (nontrivial group); this follows because the Fourier support must generate the dual group. Under base-field extension q->q^e the pole and spectral bounds scale linearly in N only for families with N comparable to q^e and the relevant degree and nondegeneracy fixed. A counting lower bound grows logarithmically with N when Q is fixed; if Q also grows, log_Q N can remain bounded.

Rank changes by at most one under f->af+b provided a!=0. Pullback along surjections preserves rank, with compatible pole values. For automata, the alphabet must generate the group and the word function be defined by the represented group element; the weighted Hankel rank is the translation rank in that model. The deterministic state upper bound assumes f is nonzero; the zero function still has a one-state deterministic automaton. Neither exact rank nor a generic approximate-rank lower bound by itself establishes arbitrary bit-circuit or public-coin communication complexity lower bounds.

Run `python verify_rigidity.py` (standard library only). It checks 270,849 degree partitions across eight field sizes, all 32,768 binary functions on C_15, 392 finite Cauchy minors, and the 244-point elliptic counterexample.

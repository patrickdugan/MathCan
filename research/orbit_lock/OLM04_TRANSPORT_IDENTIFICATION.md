## 1. An unknown two-frame model

Let H be a finite group acting on a finite set Ω of size m. A known input is s∈H, and an observed output g∈Ω is generated by

\[
F_{\sigma,B}(s)=\sigma s B,
\quad \sigma\in H,\quad B\in\Omega.
\]

Here B is the unknown reference object, and σ is the unknown change of output frame. The hypothesis family is \(\mathcal F=\{(\sigma,B)\mid\sigma\in H,B\in\Omega\}\). Distinct pairs can represent identical functions on a restricted input distribution, so identifiability always refers to the chosen input law μ.

For the six-vertex perfect-matching action, H=S₆, |H|=720, |Ω|=15, and the unreduced hypothesis space has 10,800 parameter pairs. The matching stabilizer has size 48. This is a **discrete structural prior** supplied to the inference algorithm; finding that structural family from activations remains another research problem.

### OLM-04A. First-example elimination

For any exact observed pair (s,g), the compatible hypotheses are precisely

\[
\{(\sigma,\ s^{-1}\sigma^{-1}g):\sigma\in H\}.
\]

There are exactly |H| of them.

**Proof.** Rearranging \(g=\sigma s B\) in the group action gives \(B=s^{-1}\sigma^{-1}g\). This object belongs to Ω, and every σ has exactly one such B. Distinct σ produce distinct parameter pairs. QED.

In S₆, one positive pair cuts the version space from 10,800 to **720** candidates, with no need to enumerate all reference matchings separately. The identity is valid even when the first pair happens to lie in a restricted matching-class training cohort.

## 2. Exact sample-ambiguity theorem

Fix a true hypothesis \(h_*\) and let independent calibration inputs \(S_1,\ldots,S_N\) follow the same law μ on H. The graph observations are exact: \(G_i=F_{h_*}(S_i)\). For each alternative \(h\), define

\[
\alpha_h=\Pr_{S\sim\mu}(F_h(S)=F_{h_*}(S)).
\]

Let \(V_N\) be the number of hypotheses that fit *every* observed pair.

### OLM-04B. Sharp expectation and uniqueness guarantee

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[V_N-1]=\sum_{h\ne h_*}\alpha_h^N},
\qquad
\boxed{\Pr(V_N>1)\le\sum_{h\ne h_*}\alpha_h^N}.
\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** A fixed false hypothesis survives precisely when it agrees with the true mapping on all N independent draws, which occurs with probability \(\alpha_h^N\). Sum the survival indicators and apply linearity of expectation. Since \(V_N-1\) is a nonnegative integer, the probability it is positive is at most its expectation. QED.

This equality yields a computable finite-sample risk certificate when an exact agreement histogram is available. It is stronger than replacing every \(\alpha_h\) by the largest false agreement \(\alpha_{\max}\), which gives the generally looser upper bound \((|\mathcal F|-1)\alpha_{\max}^N\).

The exact SMC-04 enumerator compares all 10,800 hypotheses against each of 720 possible input permutations. For a six-vertex truth under uniform input on the whole group, its maximum false agreement is **7/15**. Restricting the calibration law to the 576 permutations whose true graph belongs to 12 selected matching classes changes the maximum to **1/2**. Only one candidate agrees on all 576 restricted inputs, giving identifiability in that cohort. The enumeration is an executable finite certificate for those specific settings; the theorem holds for arbitrary finite H, Ω and μ.

## 3. Noise and an exact pairwise Chernoff certificate

Now independently corrupt each observed graph with probability ε by replacing it with an **independent uniform draw from all m graph states**. For a candidate h let

\[
R_h=\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf1\{G_i=F_h(S_i)\}
\]

be its score. For \(0\le\varepsilon<1\), maximum-likelihood inference with a uniform prior is equivalent to maximizing \(R_h\), since the probability of each observation is \(1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon/m\) under a prediction match and \(\varepsilon/m\) otherwise.

Put \(a=1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon/m\), \(b=\varepsilon/m\).

### OLM-04C. Robust pairwise and union guarantees

For any false candidate with agreement α,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(R_h\ge R_{h_*})\le
\left[1-(1-\alpha)(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\right]^N.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently the probability that **any** false candidate reaches at least the true score is at most

\[
\boxed{\min\left\{1,\sum_{h\ne h_*}
\left[1-(1-\alpha_h)(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\right]^N\right\}.}
\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Define \(W_i=\mathbf1\{G_i=F_{h_*}(S_i)\}-\mathbf1\{G_i=F_h(S_i)\}\). If the two predictions agree, W is zero. Otherwise W equals +1 with probability a, −1 with probability b, and zero for each of the other m−2 graph outputs. Thus its unconditional one-step probabilities are \(p_+=(1-\alpha)a\), \(p_-=(1-\alpha)b\), and \(p_0=1-p_+-p_-\). The W_i are iid. For t≥0, Markov's inequality gives

\[
\Pr\left(\sum_i W_i\le0\right)
\le\left(p_0+p_+e^{-t}+p_-e^t\right)^N.
\]

The minimum occurs at \(t=\tfrac12\log(p_+/p_-)\) when b>0. Its value is \((p_0+2\sqrt{p_+p_-})^N\), equal to the bracket in (2). When b=0 take t→∞, yielding \(\alpha^N\). Summing over false candidates proves (3). QED.

The finite histogram yields a numeric noise-risk upper bound of **0.005219** for N=24 and ε=1/4, and **0.000351** for N=32. These are conservative failure bounds for a fixed true hypothesis and the restricted input law. At ε=1, observations reveal no information about h and the bound becomes vacuous.

## 4. Gauge-covariant learning

Suppose a global vertex relabeling \(\tau\in H\) changes all observed outputs from g_i to τg_i, leaving the input permutations s_i unchanged. The family transforms as

\[
\boxed{F_{\tau\sigma,B}(s)=\tau F_{\sigma,B}(s).}
\tag{4}
\]

Every compatibility score is therefore preserved under the hypothesis bijection \((\sigma,B)\mapsto(\tau\sigma,B)\), for both exact-data version spaces and noisy maximum-consensus sets.

**Proof.** The group action is associative. Equality \(F_{\sigma,B}(s_i)=g_i\) holds precisely when \(\tau F_{\sigma,B}(s_i)=\tau g_i\). Hence all equality indicators and their sums remain identical under the bijection. QED.

This is a **metamorphic correctness test** for alignment inference: a model should recover a coherently transformed frame after common output relabeling, while reporting the same posterior ambiguity and confidence.

## 5. Independent-input lower bound and its scope

If a hidden hypothesis is uniform among M parameter pairs and a learner selects the query permutations independently of that hidden hypothesis, each exact query returns at most m possible graph labels. An adaptive deterministic decision tree of N queries has at most \(m^N\) leaves. Uniform-prior exact identification therefore has probability at most \(\min\{1,m^N/M\}\); certainty requires \(m^N\ge M\).

For the 10,800 six-vertex candidates, \(15^3=3375<10800\) and \(15^4=50625>10800\). Under this *independent-query* contract, four examples are information-theoretically necessary for uniform guaranteed recovery.

**Important cohort qualification:** the SMC-04 family-heldout evaluation samples permutation inputs conditional on a matching-class subset determined by the secret model. This input selection itself may carry information about the secret. The independent-query counting bound does **not** apply to that observational cohort. The exact agreement and Chernoff certificates above remain valid because they condition on its explicitly specified iid input law for each fixed true hypothesis. The observed success after fewer than four examples is consistent with this distinction.

## 6. Experimental evidence and boundaries

[Compactification-Theory SMC-04](https://github.com/MoralityLabAI/Compactification-Theory/tree/research/smc01-synchronized-mixing-20261115/research/synchronized_mixing_v1) executes exact-frame identification using one positive calibration pair to eliminate the reference matching for every candidate frame, with a 10,800-hypothesis maximum-consensus fallback under independent graph corruption.

Across 180 independent calibration trajectories conditional on three fixed hidden models, the exact method identified the full frame/reference pair in 180/180 trials by N=10 with no corruption; at 25% corruption, it identified the truth in 174/180 at N=12 and 180/180 at N=24. Each heldout test uses all 144 permutations from three completely excluded matching classes. A recovered correct model attains the 5/6 Bayes balanced-accuracy ceiling under the matched independent-negative test law.

A negative calibration control independently resamples graph labels while retaining the calibration input cohort. It obtains about 50.9% mean heldout balanced accuracy over 60 independent control runs with 12 examples, which serves as a check against accidental training label leakage.

The attached [risk checker](transport_identification_bounds.py) validates the exact combinatorial histogram arithmetic and provides independent finite-precision estimates of the Chernoff union bound. The full hypothesis generation, exact group-action audit, source hashes and trial outputs live in the SMC-04 applied repository.

**Unproven extension:** identification of unknown transforms outside the pre-specified symmetric-group family, scaling beyond six vertices without enumerating the group, adaptation to learned SAE feature supports, and discovery of a representation's gauge class from a pretrained model. Those require separate experiments.

# Zone certificates, structural indexing and incremental distance updates

ALG-DESCENT-15 companion. Elementary written proof, no Lean check. Fix a finite acyclic Horn rule graph, goal G, known facts S, and nonnegative residual rule costs a. Rule identities and scope are immutable.

For any zone Z with G in Z and S disjoint from Z, define its entry boundary C(Z) as the rules with head in Z and all premises outside Z. Any derivation of G from S must first derive a fact in Z. Its first such rule belongs to C(Z). Thus deleting C(Z) disconnects the goal from S. This argument does not require Z to be defined by the current residual distance threshold. It applies unchanged to another state T with T disjoint from Z. If T intersects Z, the sufficient condition no longer applies; unavoidability can still be checked separately by deletion/reachability.

Let H_h be the set of rule IDs having head h and P_h the set of rule IDs mentioning h as a premise. Then
    C(Z) = (union_{h in Z} H_h) minus (union_{h in Z} P_h).
The identity is immediate by membership. Head and premise incidence sets are immutable and can be represented exactly by bitsets. A root-local memoized zone boundary is independent of both residual costs and the state; reuse it only under the same immutable graph scope. The charge delta=min_{r in C(Z)} a_r must be recomputed for each residual vector. If positive, subtract delta from each member and accumulate the charge. The ordinary unavoidable-cut proof establishes admissibility. Stop on no positive cut or a specified finite refinement rule; each positive step zeros at least one positive cost, so at most |R| positive rounds. Greedily selecting a larger current delta from another zone need not maximize the final accumulated admissible bound; later cuts may compete for residual costs.

Incremental hmax lemma. Suppose residual costs are changed on rule subset C while S stays fixed. Let D contain the heads of C and every descendant of those heads in the premise-to-head dependency DAG. For h outside D, neither its deriving-rule costs nor the distances of any of its premises change, by induction over the topological order. For h inside D, recompute the same hmax recurrence in topological order, retaining distance zero for known facts; all earlier inputs are either unchanged or already recomputed. This equals a full recomputation at every coordinate. A conservative D may include redundant descendants without harming correctness. For a changed known set, this lemma alone is insufficient; recompute or additionally mark descendants of changed known facts.

Because the exact indexed threshold method selects precisely the same ordered rule IDs, delta and residual distances as the full-scan method, it produces the same bound at every refinement. With identical deterministic search and dominance rules, the entire search trace and incumbent history are unchanged. This conditional equivalence does not extend to selection of a different transferred zone, which remains admissible but can alter priority.

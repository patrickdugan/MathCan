# Lazy refinement and exact expansion-order equivalence

ALG-DESCENT-14 companion. Paper argument, no Lean check. Fix a finite search graph, additive costs, admissible final bound B(s), and a finite monotonically nondecreasing refinement sequence b_0(s)<=...<=B(s), each admissible. Assume deterministic successors, verified incumbent updates only upon expansion or generated goals, exact best-g dominance and reopen, and pruning iff g+b>=U. Eager computes B for every admitted state. Lazy stores its present b and selects the minimum lexicographic key (g+b,b,g,insertion order). When the selected state's bound is incomplete, refine it and rekey or prune, without expanding or updating the incumbent; otherwise expand. Keep all unresolved states, preserve relative insertion order on refinement, assign fresh order to strictly cheaper admissions. Suppose refinement completes in finitely many steps and no refinement budget interrupts selection.

Claim: eager and lazy expand the same states with the same prefix costs and paths (given the same initial incumbent, deterministic transition tie handling and incumbent update policy). At each common expansion boundary, eager's OPEN is exactly the subset of lazy's OPEN with g+B<U (possibly after ignoring lazily retained states that eager pruned). Each corresponding state has the same g and relative insertion order. This holds initially. Refinement preserves this subset and never prunes a member since b<=B. When lazy selects a fully refined state x with g+B(x)<U, it has the smallest current key among its OPEN. Every other eager-eligible state has current key no larger than its final key; hence x has the smallest final key among eager-eligible states. Lexicographic monotonicity in b and preserved relative insertion order handle ties. If eager has no unresolved eligible state, lazy can only refine and prune, and terminates without further expansions. Expansion of x gives identical successors, goal/FF incumbent updates and best-g changes. States retained only by lazy may affect absolute insertion counters but not their relative order among corresponding states. Pruning by coarse b can only remove a state eager would remove. Induction gives the claim.

The above does not assert equal certificates at equal expansion budgets: an unfinished lazy bound can be strictly weaker than B. If stopped in a refinement, retain its last certified bound. At any interruption L=min(U,min_OPEN(g+b)) is sound under ordinary complete-frontier assumptions. A separate work cap may cause lazy to halt before the next eager-equivalent expansion. If learned refinement decisions allow expansion before final bound, change priority, or omit states, the equivalence claim no longer applies; admissible-bound correctness can still hold if coverage is maintained.

V14 verified on 36 fresh public weighted Horn graphs: both lazy variants exactly match eager expansion traces and incumbent histories on all 36 roots. OOD incremental lazy bound scans 11106.17 vs eager 27825.71; root-average median time 3.606 vs 6.817 ms; same 18.75 mean capped expansions, all 24 OOD optima certified. Optimized hmax remains faster at 1.965 ms but has 30.33 capped expansions, 23/24 certified. Scope: deterministic synthetic search, not general computational speedup or proof-system result.

Future: a cut C certified unavoidable from known set S also remains unavoidable from any subset S' of S, provided initial facts and rule/goal scope are unchanged: reachability with C deleted is monotone in available facts. For supersets, unavoidability must be checked again. This allows reusing cut sets as candidates for ancestor or related-state bounds, but residual charge must be recomputed against that state's current residual costs, and no cut may be charged without a valid unavoidability certificate. Neither full cost-to-go nor a prior state-specific bound transfers automatically to arbitrary supersets. See DataGoblin PR #89.

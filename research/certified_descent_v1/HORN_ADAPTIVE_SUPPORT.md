# Adaptive admissible refinement and live Horn proof support

V18 companion; written proof, no Lean check. In finite acyclic monotone Horn planning with positive costs, let J(S) be the minimum continuation cost, b(S)<=J(S) a current certified lower bound, U the cost of a verified incumbent, and g(S) a prefix cost. A refinement may replace b by a stronger certified lower bound, or be skipped. Expanding a selected state using an incomplete bound is sound: legal successors enumerate all remaining proof continuations and preserve the lower-bound coverage of the unresolved frontier. Pruning remains justified only when g+b>=U or by sound exact-state best-g dominance. Reopening on cheaper arrival, retaining unexpanded frontier coverage and retaining valid partial bounds on reopen are required. Skipping refinement can alter priorities and expansions; no equivalence to the fully refined eager trace is asserted. At any completed event, L=min(U,min_OPEN(g+b))<=J(initial)<=U. Interrupt before a new atomic event or retain the prior certified snapshot; do not discard unresolved states. In the V18 finite experiment every partial bound and policy decision is independently audited. All 216 cases certify, but none of the adaptive policies beat packed hmax on elapsed time.

## Live support restriction for V19 (unbenchmarked)

Let A(S) be the unknown-fact backward cone of the goal: start from an unknown goal; for each unknown fact in the cone include every unknown premise of every deriving rule; stop recursion at known facts. If the goal is known, the cone is empty. Consider any successful continuation from S. Extract a subproof by following the concrete deriving rule of the goal and recursively following its unknown premises to rules earlier in the continuation, stopping at S. All extracted heads lie in A(S). Execute these rules in original topological order. Every premise is known or was retained earlier. The extracted proof reaches the goal and has no greater cost; omitted positive-cost steps strictly reduce cost. Therefore some optimal proof uses only heads in A(S).

The statement also holds dynamically. Take an optimal continuation and its first action. If its head is outside A(S), the extraction removes this action and yields a strictly cheaper proof, a contradiction. After the first action, an optimal suffix is optimal from the new proved set (additive nonnegative costs, exact rule registry). Repeat the argument. Thus enumerating only applicable rules with heads in the current A(S) preserves the exact continuation optimum. In this acyclic setting A(S) can be computed by descending head order, accumulating unknown prerequisites for included heads. If S grows, A(S) can only shrink: a path to an unknown ancestor that survives the larger known set also survives the smaller. Static ancestor filtering is a weaker control.

This removes legal but unnecessary branches from proof search, rather than charging extra work for stronger bounds. It does not establish that state-dependent computation is faster, that two arbitrary states with the same cone are interchangeable, or that proof paths can be discarded. Exact state keys, rule identity, proof replay and frontier coverage remain required. For a portable checker, bind initial facts, goal, immutable rule table and the support certificate to the same scope. A future independent audit should recompute A(S) from the rule table, verify the filtered Bellman optimum against the full graph, and count both saved transition/heuristic work and overhead. All claims here concern the monotone synthetic proof-planning domain, not general logic or DLP.
